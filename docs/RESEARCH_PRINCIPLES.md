# Research principles

Azvela's research direction includes AI, distributed systems, computational efficiency and human-computer interaction. We distinguish an interesting hypothesis from a measured result and a measured result from a general conclusion.

## Research record

For a substantive experiment, record the question, hypothesis, baseline, exact source revision, environment and hardware, dataset or workload provenance, measurement procedure, metrics, uncertainty, and relevant negative results. Identify deviations from the plan and distinguish synthetic tests from real workloads.

## Comparisons

Compare like with like: model revision, precision, workload, hardware, decoding settings and evaluation methodology. Report limitations, variance and material exclusions. Do not extrapolate a narrow result into universal equivalence, frontier-level capability or production readiness without evidence.

## Reproducibility and disclosure

Where licensing, privacy and security allow, publish enough detail for independent reproduction. Use checksums and versioned artifacts where appropriate. Label inaccessible evidence and unexecuted tests explicitly. Never fabricate benchmark outputs or attribute generated data to real experiments.

Release and publication require the appropriate human authority and review of risks, data rights and disclosures. A technical pass is scoped to the tested conditions.
